Septic around Summerville

Summerville has grown fast, and much of that growth has been in neighborhoods connected to public sewer. Outside those neighborhoods, septic is everywhere. Rural Dorchester County, communities like Ridgeville, Knightsville and Givhans, and much of the surrounding countryside rely on septic systems, along with plenty of older homes closer to town.

Many of those properties sit on larger lots, which often have space for a conventional system. That's a real advantage, but it also means systems are easy to forget about. A tank that hasn't been pumped in ten or fifteen years is common here, and it's one of the most frequent causes of drain field failure we see.

Soils and water tables inland

Summerville sits farther inland than Charleston or Mount Pleasant, but the water table can still be high, especially in low-lying areas near the Ashley River, Sawmill Branch and the many swamps and wetlands in the region. Soils vary from sandy, fast-draining ground to heavier soils that drain slowly and hold water after rain.

Those differences matter when a drain field fails. A replacement field may need to be larger, placed elsewhere on the lot, or built as a raised or pumped system, depending on what the state's site evaluation finds. Knowing your soil and water table early helps you plan and budget.

Inland or not, Summerville gets its share of heavy summer storms and tropical rain. Drain fields in low spots can slow down for days afterward. If yours does, ease off on laundry and long showers until the ground dries, and call us if the problem doesn't clear up.

Trees, roots and older systems

Summerville is known as the Flower Town in the Pines, and those pines, along with the live oaks and other big trees across the area, are hard on septic systems. Roots look for water and will work their way into older clay or cracked lines and tank joints. If you've had slow drains that come back after being cleared, roots are a likely culprit.

For older systems, a camera inspection of the lines and a check of the tank and baffles can tell you whether you're looking at a simple repair or a bigger problem, before it turns into a backup.

When Summerville homeowners call us

Around Summerville, a lot of our work is routine pumping on larger rural lots, along with repairs on systems that have been in the ground for twenty or thirty years. Older concrete tanks often have worn or missing baffles, and cracked clay lines are a common entry point for roots. Catching those problems during a pumping visit is far cheaper than dealing with them after a backup.

We also work with families buying homes in the area's fast-growing outlying communities. If the property is on septic, ask the seller for the pumping history and permit records, and schedule an inspection before closing. It's one of the most useful things you can do to protect a new purchase.

FAQ

Septic questions from Summerville homeowners

How long do septic systems last around Summerville?

A well-maintained system commonly lasts 20 to 30 years or more. Regular pumping and keeping roots and heavy loads away from the lines make the biggest difference.

Do you serve rural Dorchester County?

Yes. Ridgeville, Knightsville, Givhans and the rural parts of Dorchester County are a core part of our service area.

Tree roots keep clogging my septic line. What can I do?

Clearing the line helps for a while, but roots come back. A camera inspection shows where they're getting in, and repairing or replacing that section of line is the lasting fix.

My tank hasn't been pumped in over 10 years. Is it too late?

Not necessarily. Pump it as soon as possible and have the baffles and drain field checked. Many systems are fine once the solids are removed, but don't wait for a backup.

Can I add a bedroom on septic?

Possibly. South Carolina sizes septic systems by number of bedrooms, so an addition can require a larger system and a permit. Check before you build.

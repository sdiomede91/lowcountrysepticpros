Know the signs

Is your septic system trying to tell you something?

Catching a problem early can save you thousands. Call us if you notice any of these:

  • Slow drains or gurgling pipesEspecially when several fixtures are slow at once.
  • Sewage odors inside or outsideA smell near the tank or drain field is a red flag.
  • Soggy ground or standing waterWet spots over the drain field when it hasn't rained.
  • Extra-green grass over the tankLush patches can mean wastewater is surfacing.
  • Backups in toilets or tubsStop using water and call us right away.

FAQ

Common septic questions

How often should I pump my septic tank?

Most households should pump every 3 to 5 years. Larger families, smaller tanks, or garbage disposals may mean more frequent pumping. We can recommend a schedule based on your system.

How do I know where my septic tank is?

Your county may have a permit record showing the location. If not, we can locate the tank for you. It's usually 10 to 25 feet from the house, in line with the main sewer pipe.

Do I need a septic inspection when buying a home?

It's strongly recommended. A septic system is out of sight, and repairs or replacement can be costly. An inspection tells you the condition of the system before you close.

Why does the Lowcountry need special septic care?

High water tables, sandy soils and heavy rain put extra stress on drain fields here. Regular pumping and quick attention to warning signs go a long way toward keeping your system healthy.

Do you offer emergency service?

Yes. If sewage is backing up into your home, stop using water and call us at (843) 555-0142.

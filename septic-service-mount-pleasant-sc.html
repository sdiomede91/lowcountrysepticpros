Septic in a mostly-sewered town

Most of Mount Pleasant is served by public sewer, and newer subdivisions are almost always connected. But not every home is. Some older homes, homes on larger lots, and properties on the edges of town still use septic systems, and the farther north you go along Highway 17 toward Awendaw and McClellanville, the more common septic becomes.

Because most of their neighbors are on sewer, Mount Pleasant homeowners with septic systems often don't know much about them. Many don't know where the tank is or when it was last pumped. If that sounds familiar, the first step is simple: find the tank, pump it, and get a baseline on its condition.

Coastal conditions

Mount Pleasant sits between the Cooper and Wando rivers and the Atlantic, with marsh and tidal creeks running through much of town. Like the rest of the coast, the water table is high and climbs further after big storms. Drain fields in low areas can slow down noticeably after a stretch of heavy rain and recover as the ground dries out.

North of town, in Awendaw and around the Francis Marion National Forest, many properties sit on larger, wooded lots. Septic systems there are often older and tucked among trees whose roots can reach into lines and tanks. Lots near the marsh may have had to use pumped or raised designs to meet state rules.

Pumped and raised systems need a little more attention than a simple gravity system. The pump, float and alarm should be checked when the tank is pumped, and the alarm should never be ignored. A pump that fails in the middle of a wet week can turn a small repair into a backup within a day or two.

Home sales and inspections

Mount Pleasant's real estate market moves quickly, and septic systems can catch buyers off guard. If a home you're considering isn't on sewer, a septic inspection belongs on your due diligence list. It's inexpensive compared with the cost of a failed drain field, and it gives you clear information while you still have time to negotiate.

Sellers benefit too. An inspection and pumping before listing lets you deal with any issues on your own terms, and a clean report is a good thing to have when a buyer's agent starts asking questions.

When Mount Pleasant homeowners call us

A lot of our Mount Pleasant calls come from people who have never had to think about septic before: new owners of an older home or a property out toward Awendaw who just learned the house isn't on sewer. If that's you, start with three things. Find out where the tank is, get it pumped and inspected so you know its condition, and set a reminder for the next pumping.

We also hear from homeowners after a renovation or a new pool, patio or driveway. Construction equipment driving over a drain field can crush lines and compact the soil, and the damage often doesn't show until weeks later. If you're planning work near your system, call before you start and we'll help you mark where everything is.

FAQ

Septic questions from Mount Pleasant homeowners

Will construction near my septic system cause problems?

It can. Heavy equipment can crush lines and compact the drain field. Call before you start a project and we'll help you mark the tank and lines so crews can stay clear.

Isn't all of Mount Pleasant on sewer?

Most of it is, but not all. Some older homes, larger lots and properties on the edges of town use septic, and septic is common north toward Awendaw and McClellanville.

I don't know where my septic tank is. Can you find it?

Yes. We can locate the tank using permit records where they exist, and probing and tracing the line from the house where they don't.

How often should I pump if I'm a light user?

Smaller households can often go five years or longer between pumpings. We'll measure the sludge level and recommend a schedule based on what we find.

Do you serve Awendaw and McClellanville?

Yes. Septic is the norm in much of Awendaw and McClellanville, and we serve both.

Septic Inspections for Home Buyers & Sellers

A septic system is buried and easy to overlook, but replacing one can cost more than a new roof. An inspection tells you exactly what you're dealing with before you close or before small problems grow.

Septic Inspections

What we check

  • Tank condition, including cracks, leaks and corrosion
  • Inlet and outlet baffles
  • Liquid and sludge levels
  • Signs of backup or surfacing wastewater in the drain field
  • Pumps, alarms and filters, if your system has them
  • A clear written report you can share with your agent or lender
Know the signs

When to get an inspection

  • You're buying a home with a septic system
  • You're listing your home and want to avoid surprises
  • You've noticed odors, wet spots or slow drains
  • You don't know the age or history of your system
FAQ

Septic Inspections questions

How long does an inspection take?

Most residential inspections take a few hours on site, depending on how easy the tank is to reach.

Does the tank need to be pumped for an inspection?

A full inspection usually includes pumping so we can see the inside of the tank and the baffles clearly. We'll explain the options when you book.

Can you work with my real estate agent's timeline?

Yes. Let us know your closing date when you call, and we'll do our best to fit your schedule.
